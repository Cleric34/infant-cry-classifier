SECTION 1: MASTER OVERVIEW (EVERYONE MUST KNOW THIS)
====================================================================================================

1. THE GOAL:
   Build an automated 4-class infant cry classifier to detect physiological and emotional states:
   - Burping: Gas discomfort / needs burping (310 recordings, ~6.4%)
   - Needs: Hunger / diaper / general distress (3,103 recordings, ~63.7% - dominant class)
   - Physical Pain: Acute pain distress (774 recordings, ~15.9%)
   - Scared: Startle reflex / sudden fear (684 recordings, ~14.0%)
   Total Dataset: 4,871 real audio recordings sampled at 22,050 Hz.

2. THE THREE MAJOR PROBLEMS SOLVED:
   a. Class Imbalance: 63.7% 'Needs' vs 6.4% 'Burping'. Naive models guess 'Needs' and fail on minorities.
   b. Data Leakage in Random Splits: Random 80/10/10 splits put same-baby recordings in train and test.
      We built AI-clustered pseudo-sessions and evaluated with StratifiedGroupKFold (no group crosses folds).
   c. Silence Padding Artifacts: Zero-padding short clips to 3.98s diluted acoustic stats with silence.
      Switching to true-length audio extraction boosted PANNs model accuracy from 53.69% to 78.94%.

3. THE WINNING ARCHITECTURE:
   Logit-Adjusted Multinomial Logistic Stacker combining:
   - 4-Way Foundation Model Neural Head (6,656-d embeddings from AST, WavLM, wav2vec2, PANNs) with Focal Loss
   - LightGBM on Scattering + Spectral + Pitch + Shape (dropping redundant MFCCs)
   - LightGBM on true-length V2CV features
   - LightGBM on full V3 features

4. THE FINAL BENCHMARK NUMBERS:
   - Accuracy: 87.66% (95% CI: [86.57%, 88.75%])
   - Macro F1: 0.7993 (95% CI: [0.7812, 0.8162])
   - Balanced Accuracy: 77.72% (95% CI: [75.76%, 79.53%])
   - Burping Recall: 49.0% (up from 31% in baseline)
   - Statistically Significant Gain: +3.57% accuracy (95% CI: [+2.65%, +4.48%], p < 0.05).

hiss without distorting temporal transients.

Second, in '21_build_cv.py' and '21_cv_utils.py', we established an honest 5-Fold Stratified Group

memorize specific baby voices. Because our filenames lacked baby IDs, we extracted deep neural embeddings
using PANNs, ran agglomerative cosine clustering to group near-duplicate recordings into pseudo-sessions,
and assigned folds with StratifiedGroupKFold such that no session ever spans train and test folds.

Finally, we evaluated statistical confidence using 2,000-sample vectorized group bootstrap intervals.
Our final model achieves 87.66% accuracy and 0.7993 Macro F1, representing a statistically significant
improvement of +3.57% over baseline (p < 0.05). Shivesh will now explain our handcrafted feature
engineering and ablation discoveries."

--- EXPECTED VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS FOR YASHIK ---

Q1: Why did you use Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) instead of standard FFT filtering?
A1: Standard discrete wavelet transform (DWT) and Fourier filtering suffer from shift-variance and
    phase artifacts around sudden acoustic spikes. DTCWT uses two parallel real DWT trees with Hilbert
    transform pairs, providing shift-invariance and directional selectivity. This cleans ambient hiss
    while preserving sharp crying onsets.

Q2: What is data leakage in audio datasets, and how did your grouping solve it?
A2: Data leakage happens when audio slices from the same infant or recording session sit in both training
    and testing sets. The model learns acoustic traits of the room or infant rather than generalized cry
    types. We grouped files using cosine similarity on deep neural embeddings and used StratifiedGroupKFold
    so that entire recording clusters stay isolated in either train or test.

Q3: Why did you report Macro F1 and Balanced Accuracy instead of just Accuracy?
A3: Because 63.7% of the dataset is 'Needs'. A naive baseline guessing 'Needs' 100% of the time gets
    63.7% accuracy with 0% recall on Burping, Pain, and Scared. Macro F1 averages the F1 scores across
    all four classes equally, strictly penalizing models that fail on minority classes.

Q4: What is master.csv and what columns does it contain?
A4: 'master.csv' (in infant_cry_dataset/cv/) is the single source of truth for all 4,871 real files.
    It contains: key, path, y (0-3 class integer), class name, group (cluster ID), old_split (historical
    train/val/test tag), and fold_r0, fold_r1, fold_r2 (fold assignments across 3 cross-validation repeats).

"Thank you, Yashik. I will explain our handcrafted feature engineering pipeline, our feature block
ablation experiments, our data augmentation strategy, and our tree booster implementations.

To capture the physical and mathematical properties of crying, we developed a 695-dimensional multi-domain
feature representation across 5 acoustic blocks in '23_features_v3.py':
1. Wavelet Scattering Transform (scat_, 234 features): Uses cascading wavelet filter banks (J=6, Q=16)
   and modulus operations. It provides mathematical stability to small time-warping deformations.
2. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (mfcc_, 351 features): Standard vocal tract spectral envelopes.
3. Spectral Descriptors (spec_, 99 features): Spectral centroid (brightness), rolloff, flatness, bandwidth.
4. Pitch & Voice Quality (pitch_, 5 features): Fundamental frequency (F0), jitter, shimmer, and Harmonics-
   to-Noise Ratio (HNR). Pain cries exhibit sharp F0 spikes and high jitter.
5. Envelope Shape (shape_, 6 features): Attack time, decay rate, and temporal energy dynamics.

In '23_eval_v3.py', we performed systematic Block Ablation Studies by removing one block at a time.
We uncovered two vital insights:
- Dropping Wavelet Scattering dropped Macro F1 from 0.7350 to 0.7206, proving scattering is essential.
- Surprisingly, dropping MFCCs INCREASED accuracy to 85.67% and Macro F1 to 0.7475, while boosting Burping
  recall from 31% to 35.2%. This proved MFCCs were introducing redundant collinearity and noise once
  higher-order scattering coefficients were present.

For augmentation in '22_build_aug_pool.py', we generated exactly 2 deterministic copies per minority clip
using time-shifting, subtle pitch-invariant stretching, and volume gain. In '22_aug_ablation.py', we tested
Policies P0 through P4 and confirmed that real data with balanced class weights (Policy P1) produced the
cleanest decision boundaries.

In '26_boost_cv.py', we trained LightGBM and GPU-accelerated XGBoost. LightGBM consistently outperformed
XGBoost because its leaf-wise tree growth handles high-dimensional imbalanced splits with superior purity.
Shriarya will now present our Deep Foundation Models and Meta-Learning Stacking Ensemble."

--- EXPECTED VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS FOR SHIVESH ---

Q1: What is the Wavelet Scattering Transform and why is it useful for audio?
A1: Wavelet Scattering cascades wavelet convolutions, complex modulus non-linearities, and spatial low-pass
    averaging. Unlike standard spectrograms or MFCCs, scattering representations are mathematically proven
    to be deformation-stable (Lipschitz continuous) and translation-invariant, capturing multi-scale cry
    timbre without overfitting to minor tempo changes.

Q2: Why did dropping MFCCs improve your LightGBM model?
A2: MFCCs (351 features) and Scattering (234 features) both capture spectral envelope shapes. Because
    scattering captures higher-order non-linear interactions, keeping 351 MFCC features created high
    dimensionality and noise. Removing MFCCs reduced feature count to 344, reducing tree split variance
    and raising Macro F1 by +0.0125.

Q3: What acoustic features distinguish 'Physical Pain' from normal 'Needs'?
A3: Pain cries exhibit elevated Fundamental Frequency (F0 > 450 Hz) due to vocal fold tension, high jitter
    (cycle-to-cycle pitch instability), high shimmer (amplitude instability), and high spectral centroid
    (acoustic harshness/brightness). 'Needs' cries show lower, more rhythmic harmonic contours.

Q4: Why was deterministic augmentation preferred over random sampling?
A4: Random sampling with replacement can duplicate a single clip 10+ times while omitting others,
    biasing the model toward individual recording artifacts. Our deterministic pool ('aug_manifest.csv')
    generates exactly 2 distinct augmented variations per source file with fixed random seeds.

"Thank you, Shivesh. I will cover our Deep Audio Foundation Models, our GPU-accelerated Neural Head,

While handcrafted features capture physical acoustics, large pre-trained foundation models capture deep

1. AST (Audio Spectrogram Transformer): 2D patch self-attention trained on AudioSet (1,536-d).

2. Microsoft WavLM Base+: Middle layers 4–8 mean+std pooling (1,536-d) -> 81.79% accuracy.
3. Facebook wav2vec 2.0: Middle layers 4–8 representation (1,536-d) -> 82.08% accuracy.

In '25_neural_cv.py', we concatenated all four embeddings into a 6,656-dimensional multimodal vector and

- Focal Loss (gamma = 2.0): Down-weights easy 'Needs' examples and heavily penalizes errors on 'Burping'.
- Mixup Augmentation (alpha = 0.2): Blends pairs of embeddings and target labels to smooth decision surfaces.
- 5-Seed Averaging: 5 random seeds x 5 folds = 25 neural runs.
This neural head achieved 55.5% Burping recall—the highest of any single model—and 0.7696 Macro F1.

In '27_stack_cv.py', we built our winning Meta-Learning Stacker. It takes out-of-fold log-probabilities
from our top-4 diverse models (mlp_concat_head, lgbm_v3_drop_mfcc, lgbm_v2cv, and lgbm_v3_default) and trains
a Multinomial Logistic Regression. We then optimized class-wise Logit Offsets strictly inside the training fold.

In '28_final_report.py', we proved the validity of our gains using 2,000-sample paired group-bootstrap
hypothesis testing. Our final model achieves 87.66% Accuracy and 0.7993 Macro F1, delivering a statistically
significant gain of +3.57% accuracy (95% CI: [+2.65%, +4.48%], p < 0.05). Thank you, and we welcome your questions."

--- EXPECTED VIVA QUESTIONS & ANSWERS FOR SHRIARYA ---

Q1: Why did AST (Audio Spectrogram Transformer) outperform speech models like wav2vec2 and WavLM?
A1: wav2vec 2.0 and WavLM are pre-trained on adult human spoken language (phonemes and grammar). Infant
    crying is non-verbal acoustic event sound with wideband shrieks and abrupt dynamic shifts. AST was
    pre-trained on AudioSet (2 million environmental sound clips), allowing its 2D spectrogram patch
    attention to capture non-speech acoustic textures much more effectively.

Q2: How does Focal Loss work mathematically?
A2: Standard cross-entropy is CE = -log(p_t). When 64% of samples are easy 'Needs' cries, gradients are
    dominated by majority samples. Focal Loss introduces a modulating factor: FL = -(1 - p_t)^gamma * log(p_t).
    With gamma = 2.0, an easy sample with confidence p_t = 0.9 has its loss scaled down by (1 - 0.9)^2 = 0.01
    (a 99% reduction), forcing backpropagation to focus on minority 'Burping' and 'Pain' errors.

Q3: How does the Stacker with Logit Offsets work without data leakage?
A3: The stacker trains a logistic regression on the out-of-fold log-probabilities of the 4 member models.
    To prevent bias toward the majority class, we grid-search an additive offset vector [o0, o1, o2, o3]
    on the training fold log-probabilities to maximize accuracy while constraining Macro F1 loss. The tuned
    offsets are then added to the test fold logits before softmax.

Q4: What is the paired group bootstrap test in 28_final_report.py?
A4: To prove our +3.57% gain was not random luck, we resample cluster groups 2,000 times with replacement.
    On each resample, we compute Difference = Accuracy(Final) - Accuracy(Baseline). The resulting 95%
    confidence interval is [+2.65%, +4.48%]. Because the interval does not cross zero, the gain is
    statistically significant at p < 0.05.
